Relevance-Core Coordination English (RCCE-1) is shorter than plain English in the local benchmark

This report summarizes the first coordination benchmark for the agent-to-agent protocol project. The test compared plain-English internal discussion against Relevance-Core Coordination English (RCCE-1) across seven benchmark cases, using estimated Claude-style token counts and three repeats per case.

Debate History

Round Who debated Method they produced What they contributed
Round 1 Sperber & Wilson, Halliday, Greenberg, Labov Drop interpersonal and framing overhead; keep explicit propositional content. Established that social tone, transitions, and rhetorical setup are usually expendable in task response.
Round 2 Chomsky, Jackendoff, Piantadosi, Prince & Smolensky Use simple active SVO, remove low-information tokens, rank constraints by recoverability. Shifted the reduction target from discourse fluff to grammatical structure and information density.
Round 3 Levinson, Bickerton, Lakoff, Montague Relevance-Core Coordination English (RCCE-1): typed messages with explicit claims, evidence, objections, revisions, and escalation. Converted the compression idea into a usable coordination protocol for agent-to-agent work.
Round 4 Judge / synthesis layer Boundary and fallback decisions for when the protocol should stay structured or escalate to plain language. Provided the safety valve that prevents overcompression from breaking nuanced cases.

Linguistic Methodology

The debate was not just “which prompt is shorter.” It used different linguistic theories as separate lenses on where communication cost comes from: discourse overhead, syntactic overhead, semantic redundancy, and coordination structure. Each round proposed a different compression target, then the benchmark checked whether that target actually reduced internal coordination cost without hurting compliance or quality.

Framework What it targets Compression rule it suggests
Sperber & Wilson Recoverable context and pragmatic inference cost Drop what the receiver can infer cheaply from shared context; keep what would force expensive repair.
Halliday Interpersonal, textual, and ideational metafunctions Zero interpersonal tone and most textual scaffolding; preserve only the ideational content and minimal cohesion.
Greenberg Universals and irreducible message categories Keep a minimal set of coordination functions; do not delete the message types needed for reference, negation, and action.
Chomsky Structural relations and movement cost Prefer flat, simple clause structure and avoid derivational complexity that does not add content.
Jackendoff Semantic mapping and parallel architecture Preserve the meaning-to-form mapping while stripping morphosyntactic decoration.
Piantadosi Information density per token Delete low-information tokens first and keep tokens that carry the most predictive payload.
Bickerton Proto-grammar and stable minimal structure Use a small grammar floor that stays parseable under compression.
Lakoff Framing and conceptual overhead Remove analogies, framing sentences, and other content-free setup language.
Prince & Smolensky Ranked tradeoffs between compression and recoverability Rank recoverability above brevity, and brevity above stylistic naturalness.
Levinson Deixis, ellipsis, and recoverable repetition Replace repeated terms with pronouns or ellipsis when the referent is obvious.
Montague Compositional meaning and semantic redundancy Do not restate what the question already encodes; let the structure carry the contrast when it is unambiguous.

Methodology

Stage What was done
Project focus The benchmark measures agent-to-agent coordination cost, not final user-facing answer compression.
Baseline Plain-English coordination, where agents exchange normal prose without the protocol schema.
Protocol under test Relevance-Core Coordination English (RCCE-1), using typed messages such as CLAIM, EVID, OBJ, REV, ASK, CONF, NEXT, and ESCALATE.
Benchmark cases protocol_rule_proposal, missing_evidence_repair, fallback_trigger, benchmark_scope_decision, reference_clarity, scope_expansion, and human_interrupt_during_execution.
Runner The run used the local fallback runner because the external Codex/Claude backend was unavailable at test time.
Measurement For each condition and case, the harness measured estimated internal token count, protocol compliance, repair turns, and a simple final-quality score, then averaged three repeats.

Round / Method Decisions

Round Decision Why it matters
Round 1 Remove social overhead and talk directly. Showed that stripping framing and face-work can shorten outputs, but it does not remove deeper structural redundancy.
Round 2 Restrict backgrounding, nominalization, and signposting. Improved compression by targeting sentence structure rather than only conversational fluff.
Round 3 Adopt controlled English for agent coordination. This became the benchmarked protocol because it keeps claims, evidence, objections, revisions, and next steps explicit in compact form.
Round 4 Keep a judge/synthesis layer for failure cases and boundaries. The judge layer is useful for deciding when to stay in protocol and when to escalate to plain language.

Plain-Language Conclusion

The protocol is a win on communication size. It makes the agent exchange shorter, but the seven-case run now shows a small quality dip in the local sample. The main caution is that it can introduce a little more repair traffic when nuance matters, especially around fallback and reference decisions.

This is a local benchmark result. It is useful as a first signal, not as final proof that the protocol will always outperform plain English in real agent work.

Verdict

QuestionAnswer
Did the protocol reduce internal communication? Yes, by about one-third.
Did it hurt the final recommendation quality? Only slightly in the local seven-case sample.
Is it perfectly frictionless? No, repair turns were slightly higher.
Overall read Promising, but still needs broader testing and quality checks.
